Abstract

Autonomous AI agents can act on people who never authorized them. This paper defends a non-entitlement thesis: identifying harm, predicting benefit, or having superior capabilities does not itself confer legitimate authority to impose a remedy. Five defeasible governance functions—welfare, non-exploitation, bounded authority, corrigibility, and long-horizon stewardship—are joined through a delegation-entitlement firewall that separates evidence, moral assessment, delegated entitlement, and remedy justification.

The research contributes philosophical countermodels, the Lifeline–Mandate emergency challenge, twelve authored adversarial cases, a reproducible educational reference gate, and a prospective comparative study design. It engages close rivals in political philosophy, agency law, and agent security; no empirical advantage or deployed security guarantee is claimed.
